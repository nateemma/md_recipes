# Croissant Dough

Puff pastry with yeast in it, which means it rises twice — once from steam between the layers and once from fermentation. Three turns only, because more layers in a yeasted dough go stodgy rather than flaky.

Spread over two days: dough and lamination on the first, shaping and baking on the second.

## Ingredients

Dough:

- 4 cups bread flour
- 1/4 cup granulated sugar
- 2 1/4 teaspoons instant yeast
- 2 1/2 teaspoons salt
- 1 1/4 cups cold whole milk
- 4 tablespoons unsalted butter, softened
Butter block:

- 10 ounces (2 1/2 sticks) cold unsalted butter
To finish:

- 1 egg beaten with 1 tablespoon milk

## Instructions

Dough:

1. Mix the flour, sugar, yeast, salt, milk and softened butter until a smooth dough forms, about 5 minutes in a mixer. Do not overwork it — this dough gets plenty of gluten development from the rolling.
2. Shape into a rectangle, wrap, and refrigerate overnight.
Butter block:

1. Beat the cold butter between two sheets of parchment into an even 7-inch square. Chill until firm but still pliable — it should bend without cracking.
Lamination:

1. Roll the dough to a 15 by 8 inch rectangle. Set the butter on one half and fold the other over, sealing the edges.
2. Roll to about 20 by 8 inches and fold in three. Chill 45 minutes. Repeat twice more, for three turns in total.
3. Chill overnight after the final turn.
Shaping:

1. Roll to 1/4 inch thick. Cut into long triangles, notch the base of each, and roll up from the base to the tip.
2. Prove on lined trays at warm room temperature (not above 78°F, or the butter melts out) for 2 to 3 hours, until visibly puffy and wobbly.
3. Brush with egg wash and bake at 400°F for 18 to 22 minutes, until deep golden.

## Notes

- Proving too warm is the commonest failure — butter leaks out and they fry rather than rise.
- Shaped croissants freeze unproved; thaw and prove overnight in the fridge.
