# Glace de Viande

Brown stock reduced by something like nine tenths, until it is a dark syrup that sets to a firm rubbery block in the fridge. A teaspoon melted into a pan sauce does more than any amount of stock cube ever will.

It is mostly waiting. Start it when you are cooking something else anyway.

## Ingredients

- 4 quarts Brown Stock (see Brown Stock)

## Instructions

1. Bring the stock to a simmer in a wide pan and skim thoroughly.
2. Reduce at a steady simmer, skimming every 20 minutes or so. As the volume drops, move to progressively smaller pans — this stops the reduction scorching on a wide hot base.
3. When it is down to about a quarter, lower the heat and watch it closely. It goes from glossy to burnt faster than seems reasonable.
4. It is done when it coats a spoon thickly, is dark and syrupy, and about 1 cup remains from 4 quarts.
5. Pour into a small container and refrigerate. It will set firm enough to cut.

## Notes

- Cut into cubes and freeze. It keeps almost indefinitely.
- Never salt it, at any stage. Reduction concentrates salt ninefold.
- Used in Sauce Foyot, and worth adding to any pan sauce that tastes thin.
