# New Mexican Spice-Rubbed Pork Tenderloin with Bourbon-Ancho Sauce and Sweet Potato Tamale

Adapted from *Bobby Flay: Chapter One*

We paid the rent at our Mesa Grill locations in New York City, Vegas, and the Bahamas with this dish-easily our most popular entrée. It really hits on so many levels. The spice rub creates a flavorful crust that has hints of heat, smoke, and earthiness. The bourbon brings in vanilla and caramel flavors, and the ancho chile is replete with spicy raisin notes. I hate talking about a dish like a fancy wine connoisseur, but I can't help myself with this one.
I'm making this tomorrow night. You should too.

## Ingredients

Bourbon-Ancho Sauce

- 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 medium red onion, finely chopped
- 2 cups plus 2 tablespoons bourbon
- 3 dried ancho chiles, soaked, seeded, stemmed, and pureed
- 6 cups chicken stock (see page 304)
- 1 cup thawed frozen apple juice concentrate
- 8 whole black peppercorns
- ¼ cup light brown sugar
- Kosher salt

Spice-Rubbed Pork Tenderloin

- 3 tablespoons ancho chile powder
- 2 tablespoons light brown sugar
- 1 tablespoon pasilla chile powder
- 2 teaspoons chile deárbol powder
- 2 teaspoons ground cinnamon
- 2 teaspoons ground allspice
- Kosher salt
- 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
- 4 pounds pork tenderloin
- Freshly ground black pepper

Sweet Potato Tamales (~10)

- 1 large or 2 medium sweet potatoes
- 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 24 dried com husks
- ¾ cup fresh (preferably) or frozen com kernels
- ½ medium onion, coarsely chopped
- Cloves from 1 head Roasted Garlic 
- 2 cups chicken stock or water
- 9 tablespoons unsalted butter, at room temperature
- 1½ cups yellow cornmeal
- 1 tablespoon honey
- ½ cup small-diced raw sweet potato
- 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
- 1 teaspoon ground cloves
- 1 teaspoon ground allspice
- 3 tablespoons maple syrup

Chive Butter (1/2 cup)

- ½ cup (1 stick) unsalted butter, cubed, at room temperature
- ¼ cup finely chopped chives
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper


## Instructions

Sauce

1. In a medium saucepan, heat the olive oil over high heat. Add the onion and cook until soft, 3 to 4 minutes. Add 2 cups of the bourbon, bring to a boil, and cook until reduced to a few tablespoons, about 6 minutes. Add the ancho puree, stock, apple juice concentrate, peppercorns, and brown sugar and cook, stirring occasionally, until reduced by half, about 20 minutes.
2. Strain the sauce through a fine-mesh strainer, then return it to the pan and cook over high heat until thick but still pourable, about 15 minutes. Add the remaining 2 tablespoons bourbon and cook for 2 minutes, just until the alcohol has mellowed, then season with salt.
(You can cool the sauce and then store it in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 1 day; just reheat it in a saucepan before serving.)

Pork Tenderloin

1. Preheat the oven to 400°F.
2. In a small bowl, stir together the ancho chile powder, brown sugar, pasilla chile powder, chile de árbol powder, cinnamon, allspice, and 1 teaspoon salt.
3. In a large sauté pan, heat the olive oil over medium-high heat. Season the pork with salt and pepper on both sides. Dredge the pork in the spice rub and tap off any excess. Place the pork in the pan and sear until golden brown on all sides, 8 to 10 minutes total. Transfer the pan to the oven and roast until the pork is medium doneness (the internal temperature should register about 155°F), about 8 minutes. Remove the pork from the oven and let rest for 5 minutes. Slice the tenderloins against the grain into 1-inch-thick pieces.

Chive Butter
1. In a food processor, combine the butter and chives and pulse until smooth. Season with salt and pepper.
2. Transfer to an airtight container and store in the refrigerator for up to 1 month. Alternatively, shape the butter into a log, wrap it in parchment paper, and store in the freezer for up to 3 months.

To serve

1. Ladle the bourbon-ancho sauce into the center of each of eight to ten large plates. Cut an opening in each tamale through the top of the corn husk, running from end to end, to expose the masa.
2. Push both ends of the tamale toward the middle to further expose the masa. 
3. Place a tamale on top of the sauce on each plate, top it with some chive butter, then divide the sliced pork evenly among the plates. 
4. Serve immediately.
