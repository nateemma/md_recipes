# Velouté

Béchamel's savoury cousin: the same roux, but built on white stock instead of milk. It is only ever as good as the stock, so this is the sauce that rewards a proper homemade one (see Chicken Stock).

Chicken velouté goes with poultry, veal with veal, fish with fish. The roux is cooked a shade longer than for béchamel — blond, not white.

## Ingredients

- 2 ounces (4 tablespoons) unsalted butter
- 2 ounces (about 1/2 cup) all-purpose flour
- 1 quart white stock — chicken, veal or fish (see Chicken Stock)
- Kosher salt and white pepper
- A squeeze of lemon juice

## Instructions

1. Bring the stock to a simmer in a separate pan and keep it warm.
2. Melt the butter in a heavy saucepan over medium-low heat, whisk in the flour, and cook for 3 to 4 minutes until the roux is the colour of straw and smells nutty. Blond, not brown.
3. Whisk in the hot stock a ladle at a time, letting each addition come together before adding the next.
4. Simmer gently for 30 minutes, skimming the skin that forms at the edge of the pan. It will reduce by about a quarter and thicken as it goes.
5. Strain through a fine sieve. Season with salt, white pepper and a squeeze of lemon.

## Notes

- Skimming matters here: the impurities that rise are what make a cloudy sauce.
- Keeps 3 days refrigerated, or freezes well.
- Derivatives: Suprême (cream), Allemande (egg yolk), Vin Blanc, Bercy, Normande, Poulette.
