# Za'atar

Both a herb and the blend named after it. Sumac gives the sourness, sesame the body, and the thyme should be a dried wild one if you can find it.

Made fresh it is green and fragrant; the dusty brown stuff in supermarket jars is usually old and heavy on the salt.

## Ingredients

- 1/2 cup dried thyme (or a mix of thyme, oregano and marjoram)
- 1/4 cup sesame seeds
- 3 tablespoons ground sumac
- 1 teaspoon coarse salt
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin (optional)

## Instructions

1. Toast the sesame seeds in a dry pan over medium heat, shaking constantly, until golden, 3 to 4 minutes. Cool completely.
2. Rub the dried thyme between your palms over a bowl to break it down and wake it up.
3. Combine the thyme, cooled sesame seeds, sumac, salt and cumin.
4. Pulse briefly in a spice grinder if you want it finer, or leave it coarse.
5. Store airtight.

## Notes

- Mix with olive oil to a loose paste and spread on flatbread before baking for man'oushe.
- Also good on labneh, roast vegetables, or a fried egg.
- Keeps 3 months; the sumac fades first.
