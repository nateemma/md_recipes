# Sauce Béarnaise

Hollandaise with a sharp reduction of vinegar, shallots and tarragon in place of the lemon. The sauce for a steak, and arguably better than its parent.

Half the tarragon goes into the reduction and half in at the end. That is what gives it both depth and freshness.

## Ingredients

Reduction:

- 1/4 cup white wine vinegar
- 1/4 cup dry white wine
- 2 shallots, finely chopped
- 2 tablespoons chopped tarragon stems
- 1 teaspoon black peppercorns, cracked
Sauce:

- 3 large egg yolks
- 1 cup (2 sticks) unsalted butter, clarified and warm
- 2 tablespoons chopped tarragon leaves
- 1 tablespoon chopped chervil or parsley
- Kosher salt and cayenne

## Instructions

Reduction:

1. Simmer the vinegar, wine, shallots, tarragon stems and peppercorns until reduced to about 2 tablespoons, 8 to 10 minutes. Strain, pressing hard, and let cool slightly.
Sauce:

1. Set a heatproof bowl over barely simmering water. Add the yolks and the strained reduction and whisk constantly until thick enough to leave a trail, 3 to 4 minutes.
2. Off the heat, add the warm clarified butter drop by drop at first, whisking, then in a thin stream once the emulsion holds.
3. Stir in the tarragon leaves and chervil. Season with salt and a pinch of cayenne.

## Notes

- With tomato purée added it becomes Choron; with meat glaze, Foyot.
- Same rescue as hollandaise: a fresh yolk in a clean bowl, broken sauce added drop by drop.
