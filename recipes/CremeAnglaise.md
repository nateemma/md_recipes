# Crème Anglaise

The other custard, and the opposite discipline to pastry cream: no starch, so it must never boil. It thickens between 175 and 185°F and scrambles not far above, which is a narrow window and the reason people are frightened of it.

A thermometer removes the fear entirely.

## Ingredients

- 2 cups whole milk (or 1 cup milk and 1 cup heavy cream)
- 1 vanilla bean, split and scraped
- 6 large egg yolks
- 1/2 cup granulated sugar
- A pinch of salt

## Instructions

1. Bring the milk to a bare simmer with the vanilla bean and seeds. Off the heat, infuse for 15 minutes, then remove the pod.
2. Whisk the yolks, sugar and salt together until pale.
3. Temper with about a third of the hot milk, whisking, then return everything to the pan.
4. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly with a wooden spoon in a figure of eight, until it thickens enough to coat the back of the spoon — draw a finger through it and the line should hold. This is 175 to 180°F. It will take 8 to 10 minutes.
5. Strain immediately through a fine sieve into a cold bowl. Straining catches any threads of cooked egg and stops the cooking.
6. Cool over an ice bath, stirring, then refrigerate.

## Notes

- If it does scramble, blitz it hot in a blender for 30 seconds. It usually comes back smooth, if a little thinner.
- Set with gelatin and lightened with cream it becomes Crème Bavaroise. Churned, it is ice cream base.
- Keeps 3 days refrigerated.
