# Fish Fumet

The one stock that must not be cooked long. Twenty-five minutes and it is finished; an hour and the bones give up compounds that turn it bitter and glue-like.

Use only lean white fish — sole, turbot, whiting, brill. Oily fish makes a fumet nobody wants.

## Ingredients

- 3 pounds bones and heads from lean white fish, gills removed
- 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 1 onion, thinly sliced
- 1 leek, white part only, thinly sliced
- 1 fennel bulb, thinly sliced (optional)
- 8 ounces mushroom trimmings
- 2 cups dry white wine
- 2 quarts cold water
- 1 bay leaf
- 6 parsley stems
- 6 white peppercorns
- 1 strip lemon peel

## Instructions

1. Rinse the bones under cold running water for 10 minutes, until the water runs clear. Remove the gills — they are bitter and bloody.
2. Melt the butter in a large pot over low heat. Add the onion, leek, fennel and mushroom trimmings and sweat for 8 minutes without colouring.
3. Add the fish bones and sweat for 5 minutes more, turning gently, until opaque.
4. Add the wine and simmer until reduced by half.
5. Add the cold water, bay leaf, parsley stems, peppercorns and lemon peel. Bring to a bare simmer, skimming.
6. Simmer for 25 minutes. No longer.
7. Strain through muslin without pressing. Cool quickly.

## Notes

- Reduced by half it becomes fish glaze, for enriching sauces.
- Freezes 3 months.
- See also: Velouté, Sauce Vin Blanc, Sauce Normande.
