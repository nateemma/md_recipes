# Sauce Robert

One of the oldest named sauces in French cooking, and still the right thing with a pork chop. Onions softened in butter, white wine and vinegar reduced over them, demi-glace, and mustard stirred in at the very end.

The mustard goes in off the heat. Boil it and it turns bitter and loses its point.

## Ingredients

- 3 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 1 cup dry white wine
- 2 tablespoons white wine vinegar
- 2 cups Demi-Glace (see Demi-Glace)
- 2 tablespoons Dijon mustard
- 1 teaspoon sugar
- Kosher salt and black pepper

## Instructions

1. Melt 2 tablespoons of the butter in a saucepan over medium-low heat. Add the onion and cook gently for 12 to 15 minutes, until soft and golden but not browned.
2. Add the wine and vinegar and reduce by two thirds, about 8 minutes.
3. Add the demi-glace and simmer gently for 15 minutes.
4. Off the heat, whisk in the mustard, the remaining butter and the sugar. Season with salt and pepper.
5. Do not let it boil once the mustard is in.

## Notes

- Add a handful of julienned cornichons at the end and it becomes Sauce Charcutière.
