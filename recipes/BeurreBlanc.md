# Beurre Blanc

From the Loire, and the great butter sauce for fish. No roux and no egg — the emulsion is held together by the butter itself, which is why it must go in cold, piece by piece, off a low heat.

Boil it and it breaks. That is the only rule.

## Ingredients

- 1/2 cup dry white wine (Muscadet if you have it)
- 2 tablespoons white wine vinegar
- 3 shallots, very finely chopped
- 2 tablespoons heavy cream (optional, for stability)
- 1 cup (2 sticks) cold unsalted butter, cut into small cubes
- Kosher salt and white pepper
- Lemon juice

## Instructions

1. Simmer the wine, vinegar and shallots in a heavy saucepan until reduced to about 2 tablespoons of syrupy liquid, 10 to 12 minutes.
2. Add the cream if using and bring to a bare simmer. It makes the sauce far more forgiving.
3. Reduce the heat to low. Whisk in the cold butter two or three cubes at a time, adding more only when the last has almost disappeared. The sauce should stay creamy and never bubble.
4. Strain out the shallots if you want it smooth, or leave them in.
5. Season with salt, white pepper and a few drops of lemon. Serve within the half hour, kept barely warm.

## Notes

- A thermos is the practical way to hold it. Over direct heat it will split within minutes.
- With red wine in place of white it becomes beurre rouge, for beef.
