# Sauce Allemande

Velouté enriched with a liaison of egg yolks and cream. Escoffier called it *sauce parisienne*, apparently out of patriotism, but the older name stuck.

The liaison is the whole trick: temper it properly and never let the sauce boil afterwards, or the yolks curdle.

## Ingredients

- 1 quart veal or chicken Velouté (see Velouté)
- 3 egg yolks
- 1/2 cup heavy cream
- 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, cold
- 1 tablespoon lemon juice
- Freshly grated nutmeg
- Kosher salt and white pepper

## Instructions

1. Reduce the velouté by about a quarter over medium heat, skimming, 15 to 20 minutes.
2. Whisk the yolks and cream together in a bowl.
3. Whisk a ladle of the hot velouté into the yolk mixture to temper it, then a second. Pour the tempered liaison back into the pan, whisking.
4. Cook over low heat, stirring constantly, until the sauce thickens enough to coat a spoon — about 3 minutes. Do not let it boil.
5. Off the heat, whisk in the cold butter. Season with lemon, nutmeg, salt and white pepper. Strain.

## Notes

- If it does break, blend it hot with an ice cube; it often comes back.
- Allemande with mushrooms and parsley becomes Sauce Poulette.
